Coach and bench-staff sanctions

What happens to the game after a coach or someone on the bench (not a player) gets a warning, a 2-minute suspension or a disqualification. Covers Liga ASOBAL, Liga Guerreras Iberdrola, Ligue Butagaz Énergie, StarLigue and ProLigue.

How to read this page
Usually
What normally happens to a team in the same minute, score and run of play, without any sanction. It is the fair comparison for every number here.
X% more or less likely
The chance after the sanction compared with the usual chance. For example, 17% against 15% usually is 13% more likely.
Clear or could be chance
With few sanctions, a difference can be luck. "Clear" means it is unlikely to be luck; "could be chance" means we can't tell it apart from normal variation.
Winning, tied, losing
The score at the moment of the sanction, from the sanctioned team's point of view.
Player reference
A player's 2-minute suspension, shown as a case where we know the team is hurt (it plays a man down).

What to keep in mind

Bench staff means everyone on the match sheet who is not a player. Sanctions come from the match reports of the Spanish federation, the French women's league and the French federation's match sheets. The LNH PDFs have no event log, so French men's games come only from the federation sheets. Those cover 2019-20 to 2022-23 and 2025-26 for StarLigue, and most of the same seasons for ProLigue; the other seasons are not downloaded yet, so read the French men's rows as partial. About one in fourteen Spanish games has no clock and counts only in the result measures. Small groups, teams in particular, move a lot from one sample to the next. These are patterns in the data: a staff member is usually sanctioned after something has gone wrong, and who has the ball at that moment matters, so a gap in the next-goal rate may reflect that and not the sanction itself.
